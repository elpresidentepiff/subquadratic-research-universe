01Executive Investment View

Quant is positioned in a part of the digital-asset market that matters to institutional adoption: interoperability between regulated financial institutions, existing payment systems, and multiple public or private ledgers. The investment case is not that one blockchain wins. It is that banks, payment networks, asset managers and corporates will operate across several ledgers and need secure orchestration, settlement and compliance between them.

Quant's product stack addresses that problem through Overledger and the Fusion Rollup — moving from an interoperability proposition into an institutional execution environment. In September 2026, The Clearing House selected Quant technology for its On-Chain Money Initiative, intended to support clearing and settlement of tokenised deposits and connect with existing US payment rails, including RTP and CHIPS. On September 26 2026, Barclays, HSBC, and five additional UK banks executed live tokenised GBP deposit payments via Quant — the first multi-bank, multi-institution live production deployment, not a pilot.

Central thesis: As money and financial assets become programmable and tokenised, the infrastructure that connects fragmented ledgers to regulated financial markets can become strategically important. Quant is a credible company-level contender in that layer. QNT is the associated digital asset — but its investment case must be underwritten separately from the quality of the company and its partnerships.

The financial system is not going to become one blockchain. It is becoming a network of banks, payment systems, tokenised deposits, stablecoins, securities ledgers and regulated digital-asset venues. Those systems need to communicate, coordinate and settle with one another. Quant is positioning itself in that connective tissue.
